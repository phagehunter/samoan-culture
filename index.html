No Family Is an Island

A study companion to Ilana Gershon's ethnography of Samoans in diaspora, by Curtis Hoffmann

This single-page study aid covers No Family Is an Island: Cultural Expertise among Samoans in Diaspora (Cornell University Press, 2012), with the Introduction and Chapters 1 to 4 in focus. It combines distant reading, interactive concept and relationship maps, with close reading: a chapter-by-chapter guide and scene-level commentary.

A section slider (Introduction to Conclusion) and group toggles re-draw every view, and a playback control animates the book section by section. Clicking a node, edge or term opens the commentary panel with summaries, thematic tags and page citations, each linking into the Guide tab of original per-section synopses.

The four views

  • Study / Exam. The landing sheet: the book in one idea, the central clash between Ben and Alofa (systems carrier against cultural broker), the cultural and acultural contrast, every starred term grouped by domain, and a chapter-by-chapter nutshell.
  • Key Terms. The vocabulary as a force-directed concept map: Gershon's analytic terms plus the Samoan and bureaucratic terms the argument turns on, linked by the relations the text draws. A gold ring marks the key terms.
  • People and Systems. Who meets whom across the social orders: families and chiefs, churches, government bureaucracies, and the community brokers between them. Red dashed edges mark a clash of reflexivities.
  • Narrative Gravity. A stream graph of what each section attends to (families, churches, systems, brokers, theory), with the focus window marked and a setting strip for the United States, New Zealand and Samoa.
